\begin{abstract}
We present Ankyra, a domain-general neuro-symbolic reasoning engine in which a large
language model \emph{proposes} a formalization and a deterministic symbolic engine
\emph{decides} it. The engine covers a family of decidable formalisms (definite Horn
logic, stratified negation, positive first-order logic with disjunction and
quantifiers, finite-domain constraints, exact arithmetic, and defeasible defaults),
each with a sound decision procedure and a declared fragment. Every answer carries a
mechanically verifiable provenance trace, and no statement enters the theory without a
verbatim quote or an explicit hypothesis tag. We report a proof-of-concept evaluation
on standard collections: the engine attains high accuracy on the slices it covers while
producing \emph{zero grounded false proofs}, and it abstains honestly
(\texttt{out\_of\_fragment}) where its committed logic cannot express a problem. We
make no state-of-the-art claim: the evaluation uses committed subsets rather than full
test splits, and we state the resulting statistical and protocol limitations explicitly.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

% TODO: expand the motivation (LLM hallucinated reasoning; verifiability; the
% propose/decide boundary). Keep the explicit scope statement below.

The central design commitment is: \emph{the LLM proposes, the engine decides}. The
language model never owns truth. Every statement it contributes is deterministically
classified as \texttt{derivable}, \texttt{cited} (supported by a verbatim quote),
\texttt{hypothesis} (knowledge absent from the text, tagged), or \texttt{rejected}. An
answer is never stronger than its grounds: a conclusion from quotations is
\texttt{proven}, one resting on assumptions is \texttt{proven\_under(H)}.

\paragraph{Contributions.} % TODO: turn into a list.
\begin{itemize}
	\item A propose/decide architecture with mechanically verifiable provenance.
	\item A family of decidable formalisms behind a \emph{declared-fragment contract},
	      with honest \texttt{out\_of\_fragment} abstention instead of silent downgrade.
	\item A gating methodology for \emph{composite} benchmarks that separates the
	      coverage (formalism) ceiling from the extraction (model) ceiling via a
	      gold-fed counterfactual.
	\item An empirical proof of concept on standard collections with a measured
	      soundness invariant (zero grounded false proofs).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Scope (explicit).} This is a proof-of-concept report, not a
state-of-the-art claim. We evaluate \emph{committed subsets} of standard collections,
describe the budget that prevents full test-split runs, and give confidence intervals.
Whole-collection coverage is unreachable by design for mixed-construct benchmarks such
as FOLIO (Section~\ref{sec:results}).

\section{System overview}

% TODO: expand. Keep this section short and factual for now.

Ankyra is an orchestrator of formalizations and decision procedures, not a monolithic
solver. A problem passes through: (0)~decomposition of the text into a theory and a
question; (1)~deterministic closure of the theory; (2)~a bounded proposal cycle (the
LLM proposes one typed step per wave); and (3)~mechanical explanation assembled from
provenance. Rules, facts and hypotheses form a base that only grows; the answer may be
revised, and each revision is recorded.

Deterministic code never interprets natural language. Normalizing identifiers and closed
enum fields the LLM has already extracted is allowed; heuristics over raw text keyed on
words or cue phrases are not. The only raw-text operations are structural witness checks
(verbatim-quote substring/span, coverage, length).

\section{Formalisms and decision procedures}

Each stage of the reasoning roadmap is a named formalism with a sound decision
procedure, entered only after an LLM-free synthetic gate and an external collection
gate.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{p{0.8cm}p{5.4cm}p{5.6cm}}
\toprule
\textbf{Stage} & \textbf{Formalism} & \textbf{Procedure} \\
\midrule
L0 & definite Horn, \texttt{is\_a}, complementary-pair negation (open world) & semi-naive forward chaining \\
L1 & stratified negation / NAF, declared closed world & stratified closure, disjointness constraints \\
L2 & positive FOL: disjunction, $\exists/\forall$, proof by cases & bounded resolution (finite-domain grounding) \\
L3 & finite-domain constraints (CSP/SAT) & in-repo finite-domain search \\
L4 & arithmetic terms and equations & exact rational evaluation / linear elimination \\
D  & defaults with specificity via \texttt{is\_a} & defeasible layer (NFA + specificity) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

A query declares the required fragment; a mismatch is reported as
\texttt{out\_of\_fragment}, never guessed. L3 and L4 are separate engines behind the
same orchestration.

\section{Evaluation methodology}
\label{sec:method}

\paragraph{Metrics.} For each collection we report \emph{kind accuracy} (a
three-way or multiple-choice label), with Wilson 95\% confidence intervals. The gate
also enforces a \emph{soundness invariant}: every determinate answer is
\texttt{proven}, and there are \emph{zero grounded false proofs} (a confidently wrong
determinate answer).

\paragraph{Two ceilings.} For a composite benchmark we separate:
\emph{coverage} (the share of gold formulas the committed logic can express and
decide; the remainder is an honest \texttt{out\_of\_fragment}) from
\emph{extraction} (the share of covered rows the LLM translates correctly). The
\emph{gold-fed} counterfactual feeds the engine the annotated formalization instead of
the LLM extraction, isolating method accuracy from language accuracy.

\paragraph{Budget and reproducibility.} There is no free LLM access; live runs are
paid. Each collection therefore enters as a small committed sample, run once, and is
re-run only on a mismatch. All LLM-free gates (synthetic suites, the gold-fed
diagnostic) are reproducible from the repository alone.

\paragraph{FOLIO (L1/L2, the composite case).} FOLIO's gold FOL spans several
formalisms at once, so it is run as in-fragment slices:
\begin{itemize}
	\item L1 negation slice (13 rows): text-fed 11/13, gold-fed 12/13.
	\item L2 tier a (45 rows): coverage 44/45 (one malformed annotation); on covered
	      rows text-fed 29/44 versus gold-fed 42/44.
\end{itemize}
The gap between gold-fed (method) and text-fed (extraction) localizes the remaining
cost to extraction; the coverage ceiling is essentially closed on this slice. The
complement of the two committed gate fragments (13 + 93 rows of the 204-row validation
split) carries constructs outside every committed stage, reported
\texttt{out\_of\_fragment}: xor, $\leftrightarrow$, multi-variable quantification,
equality, function terms, axiom schemas.

\paragraph{AR-LSAT (L3).} Gold-fed 27/27 over real games; live eval 23/30 (76.7\%),
0 \texttt{grounded\_mismatch}.

\paragraph{GSM8K (L4).} Exact arithmetic makes arithmetic errors impossible by
construction. Synthetic 37/37; hand-encoded gold 8/8; live eval 38/40 (the two
non-correct rows are annotated reference errors: one dataset error, one ambiguity).

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

% TODO: this is the section that must stay brutally honest. Keep all of it.

\paragraph{Sample size and statistics.} Every real-collection number rests on a
small sample (N = 13, 30, 40, 42, 44, 300), so the confidence intervals are wide
(Table~\ref{tab:results}); on the small slices they overlap plausible baselines, and
they do not support a state-of-the-art claim. Detecting a difference of about five
percentage points at $p\approx0.95$ with power $0.8$ would require on the order of
$400$ examples per arm.

\paragraph{Run-once and nondeterminism.} The live path invokes an LLM whose provider is
nondeterministic even at temperature zero. Runs are executed once (budget policy) and
repeated only on a mismatch, so we report no variance estimate and make no
significance test.

\paragraph{Subsets, not test splits.} We evaluate committed samples: ProntoQA-OOD tier
a (42 of 5\,800), AR-LSAT eval (30 of $\sim$230), GSM8K eval (40 of $\sim$1\,319),
ProofWriter Tier D (300 of 6\,368 theories). Full collections were not run because there
is no free LLM access and the runs are paid out of pocket; this is a deliberate scope
boundary, not an unreported failure.

\paragraph{Soundness is relative to the formalization.} The engine is sound given the
theory it is handed; the compiler of natural language into that theory is the LLM. An
extraction error can therefore produce an \emph{abstention}, or, in principle, a
\texttt{proven} answer about the wrong formalization. We measure \emph{grounded false
proofs} (zero on every reported slice) and mitigate extraction with quote grounding,
a conditional-quote guard, a grounded-refutation guard, and domain-declaration guards.
This is not a proof of end-to-end truth against the world.

\paragraph{Composite benchmarks have no single headline.} For FOLIO, whole-collection
coverage is unreachable by design: equality, function terms, xor, biconditional,
multi-variable quantification and axiom schemas lie outside every committed stage, and
full first-order entailment is only semi-decidable. We therefore report stratified
slices and an explicit \texttt{out\_of\_fragment} count, never a whole-collection score.

\paragraph{No head-to-head baselines.} Published baseline numbers use different
collections, splits, protocols and front-end models, so they are not directly
comparable; we do not run a shared-front-end comparison. The contribution is a
soundness and verifiability argument, with accuracy as supporting evidence.

\paragraph{Model and provider drift.} Numbers are tied to the extraction provider used
at run time; a different or updated model would change the text-fed columns. The
gold-fed and synthetic columns are model-independent.

\paragraph{Defeasible defaults.} Only a synthetic gate is reported; a real
defeasible/NLI collection remains future work.

\section{Conclusion}

% TODO: short restatement of the thesis, the soundness-and-verifiability claim, and
% the honest scope (proof of concept, not state of the art).

We presented a neuro-symbolic engine in which the LLM proposes and a deterministic
engine decides, covering a family of decidable formalisms behind a declared-fragment
contract. On committed slices of standard collections the engine achieves high
accuracy while producing zero grounded false proofs, and it abstains honestly where its
logic cannot express a problem. The evaluation is a proof of concept; the limitations
above define the boundary of what these results support.
